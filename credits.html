Credits

Solar Time combines original application design with credited scientific references, public space imagery, licensed textures, music, and project-specific artwork. This page identifies the people and sources behind the experience.

Created by

Life User designed and maintains the application, interface, camera system, rendering, interactions, explanatory writing, and the original visual presentation.

Scientific purpose

Solar Time is an educational and ambient visualization. It is not a precision ephemeris, navigation system, or mission-planning tool.

Astronomy and orbital references

Planetary positions are based on approximate and long-term orbital models published by NASA/JPL. Reference states are checked offline against JPL Horizons. The Moon, Europa, Pluto, eclipses, and alignment views use dedicated project models. The released application does not contact JPL Horizons while it runs.

  • NASA/JPL approximate positions of the planets provides the orbital elements and correction terms used to calculate planet positions across Solar Time's supported dates.
  • NASA/JPL Horizons supplies reference vectors used by the offline validation suite to check selected dates. Solar Time does not query Horizons while visitors use the site.
  • NASA/NSSDCA planetary fact sheets provide physical reference values such as body dimensions, gravity, temperature, and rotation data shown in the information cards.
  • Astronomy Engine by Don Cross provides precision routines for the Moon and Europa positions and for solar-eclipse searches. It is redistributed under the MIT License.

Planet imagery and textures

Earth imagery includes NASA Earth Observatory Blue Marble and Black Marble references. Venus uses NASA Magellan radar imagery for its surface. Several planet and Moon maps are derived from the Solar System Scope texture collection by INOVE under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license. Each map is prepared into multiple levels of detail for the Solar Time renderer.

  • NASA Earth Observatory · Blue Marble Next Generation is prepared as the daylight surface of Earth. It is a global composite rather than a live satellite image.
  • NASA Earth Observatory · Earth at Night provides the 2016 Black Marble source used for lights on Earth's night side. It is an annual composite, not live electrical activity.
  • Natural Earth · Admin 0 Countries supplies the public-domain boundary vectors used for country hover outlines. Solar Time keeps only supported regions and simplifies them for lightweight display.
  • NASA/JPL-Caltech · Venus Magellan radar mosaic forms the cloud-free Venus surface used by the visualization. It is radar-derived simulated colour, not a visible-light photograph.
  • Solar System Scope / INOVE texture collection supplies licensed maps for Mercury, Venus's atmosphere, the Moon, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune, and Saturn's rings.
  • Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International is the license governing the Solar System Scope maps. Solar Time retains the required attribution and source links on this page.

Original and AI-assisted artwork

The space background, procedural stars, dust, haze, fallback materials, corona, rings, and supporting effects are project-authored unless another source is identified. Sun, Pluto, Uranus, and Europa maps are artistic spherical reconstructions created for Solar Time with OpenAI ImageGen from named NASA or USGS references. Flight particles and Saturn ring-tour debris are also original generated assets made for this project. These images are illustrative and are not measured all-sky maps or photographs.

Source preparation and limitations

Large maps are resized into several levels of detail so distant bodies can load a smaller texture before a close view uses higher resolution. This processing preserves the source artwork but does not create new measured surface detail.

Earth's cloud and night-light layers are fixed composites rather than live weather or electrical activity. Saturn's ring map is converted into a radial colour-and-transparency profile, while procedural materials may appear temporarily during loading or in compatibility mode.

Planet sizes, orbit spacing, texture colours, unobserved terrain, event presentation, and travel effects may be adjusted for legibility. Alignment dates generated by Solar Time are model results and are not named official astronomical events.
