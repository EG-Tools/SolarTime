How these notes are prepared

Each article starts from a behavior that can be reproduced in Solar Time. It then separates three things that are easy to confuse: an astronomical quantity, the app's simplified model of that quantity, and the visual presentation used to make it legible on a screen. External references support general astronomy; statements about the interface and rendering describe Solar Time's own implementation.

The notes are reviewed when the related feature changes. They do not claim observatory precision, and they identify cases where visual scale, long-range approximation, or cinematic timing differs from physical reality. Corrections can be sent to Lyrikey@Naver.com.

Start with an experiment

  1. Return to the overviewOpen Solar Time and press 0 so scale and camera comparisons begin from a known composition.
  2. Change one controlAdjust time, true-size mode, a moon size, or constellation lines while leaving the other controls unchanged.
  3. Compare the explanationUse the matching note to decide whether the change represents astronomy, presentation, or a transition between the two.

Scope: Solar Time is designed for education, exploration, and ambient viewing. Confirm event times and precise positions with a professional ephemeris when accuracy matters.
