INTERACTION DESIGN · CAMERA AND PARTICLES

Camera travel and the sense of depth

Opening, warp, and ring travel share a visual language but solve different motion problems. Treating them as one effect would make their handoffs less continuous, not more.

Written by Life UserPublished 2026-10-16Reviewed 2026-10-17

Opening moves through an existing scene

The opening establishes scale by allowing nearby particles to cross the frame while the solar system grows from a distant point into the saved or default composition. Particles need varied depth and individual fade timing; otherwise the whole layer appears to switch on or off.

The ending is a state handoff. Camera position, lens, bank, body scale, texture detail, clouds, lighting, orbit lines, and labels must converge without a visible snap. Expensive textures can be prepared early, but should not become fully visible until their blend is ready.

Warp lets particles carry the speed

Warp cannot move the camera through an unlimited physical scene, so the particle stream supplies much of the perceived velocity. Population begins lightly, reaches its strongest interval later, then decreases before arrival. Tail length, width, brightness, and lateral origin vary so the field does not look copied.

A direction is chosen for one journey. Continually changing particle roll looks like unstable steering and adds calculation without a useful cue. A stable direction with a meaningful chance of near-zero roll produces variety while remaining comfortable.

The warp-to-opening bridge overlaps motion

If warp ends before opening begins, the screen pauses. If both fields reach full density together, the number of stars suddenly doubles. Solar Time overlaps a fading warp tail with a gradually appearing opening field while bringing their apparent flow speeds closer.

Late warp particles should pass the viewer or recede naturally. Their opacity follows remaining life rather than disappearing only because a target particle count has fallen. Count-based deletion is the source of many “lights off” endings.

Saturn and Uranus travel follow geometry

Saturn's rings make camera clearance obvious. A straight interpolation from an arbitrary tracked view can cut through the ring plane or force the target angle suddenly. A curved route blends current camera velocity into the approach, so starting close to Saturn does not replace the user's motion with a canned direction.

Canceling is another handoff. Saturn, the rings, and the other planets must return through the same scale progression. If surrounding planets become points first, the system seems to reassemble in separate pieces.

Saturn and Uranus share the same travel structure, with different ring profiles and fragment appearance. From the overview, T chooses the nearer available destination. Once the loop begins, another T transfers directly to the other ring system; further presses keep alternating. During transit, T is ignored so a second request cannot replace the active approach.

The route uses curves and clearance checks around displayed bodies, including on return. Keeping the departing ring visible until it has receded helps the two destinations feel like parts of one continuous scene. Use 0 for the default composition or Escape for the pre-journey view; dismiss an open card first if it consumes Escape.

Cinematics do not change astronomical state

A cinematic may rotate the viewing frame, move the camera, alter presentation scale, and choose particle cues. It should not change simulation time or regenerate star coordinates. When a hidden browser tab resumes, one monotonic progress value should settle the renderer into a valid camera state.

Comfort rule: continuity has priority over maximum spectacle. A predictable curve is preferable to preserving an old direction and then making a sudden correction.

Try it in Solar Time

  1. Start the default opening and watch particle layers appear individually.
  2. Press W from several camera angles and compare the direction and population ramp.
  3. Press T to enter the nearer ring system. Wait for the loop, then press T again to visit the other planet; compare the two ring fields.
  4. Switch browser tabs during a cinematic and return. The final camera should remain recoverable with 0.
