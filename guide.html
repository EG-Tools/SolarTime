Guide to Solar Time

Solar Time turns the current time into a navigable view of the solar system. This guide explains how to move through the scene, change simulation time, follow a planet, use cinematic travel, and keep the experience running smoothly.

Start here

  1. Let the opening finishThe solar system settles into your saved or default view. You can skip or replay cinematic travel later.
  2. Move the cameraDrag to rotate, use the wheel to move forward or backward, and select a body from the list along the bottom.
  3. Choose how time flowsKeep Real time on for the live clock, or turn it off and set a faster rate in hours, days, or years per second.

Good first view: press 0 whenever you want to return to the home composition, then select Earth or Saturn from the footer.

Move and compose the camera

Orbit the view

Left-drag to rotate around the current focus. Middle-drag pans the composition without changing the viewing angle.

Distance and lens

Use the mouse wheel to move forward or backward. Right-drag changes the lens from telephoto to wide angle while keeping the camera position.

Follow a body

Double-click a planet or major moon to follow it. The camera approaches smoothly and continues to track the body as simulation time advances.

Save a composition

Camera slots 1–3 store favorite viewpoints. Open a numbered slot to save, recall, or remove its view.

Default View restores the overview without erasing saved camera slots. The forward/back control can switch the wheel between a flat zoom and physical camera travel, which changes the sense of depth around nearby bodies.

Control simulation time

The clock at the top follows the selected region. With Real time enabled, the model follows the current clock. Turn Real time off to use the lower slider. Click the unit button to cycle through hours, days, and years; Solar Time remembers the unit you last selected.

The available ranges are 1 minute–24 hours, 1–365 days, and 1–20 years advanced per real second. Planets follow elliptical paths, so their apparent speed varies across an orbit. The Moon and Europa continue around their parent planets at the selected simulation time.

Accuracy note. Solar Time is an educational and artistic visualization. Long-term perturbations and small distance changes are simplified, so confirm observation dates, navigation, and eclipse predictions with an authoritative ephemeris.

Select and inspect celestial bodies

Use the footer list to select the Sun, planets, Moon, Europa, and Pluto. The information card shows useful facts and body-specific controls. The left and right arrow keys move through the same list; when tracking is active, the camera transfers smoothly to the new target.

The normal overview adjusts sizes and orbit spacing so the whole system remains readable. True size ratio reduces those visual adjustments for comparison. Display Size changes individual bodies, while Orbit Spacing changes their visual separation. These controls affect presentation rather than rewriting the selected simulation date.

Earth and Venus include cloud controls. High-resolution surfaces are loaded as you approach a body, so a sharper texture may replace the first image after a short moment.

Opening, warp, and Saturn ring travel

Press T to start the selected travel sequence. The UFO control chooses the opening style. A journey may pass through a departure, continuous flight, and arrival sequence before restoring the solar-system view.

Saturn ring travel approaches Saturn, enters the ring field, and continues around the rings. Press Space to pause the cinematic camera. During the ring tour, a paused view can still be rotated for screenshots; during warp, manual camera control remains locked so the flight direction stays coherent. Press Space again to continue.

Cinematic travel temporarily locks settings that would change planet size, orbit spacing, eclipse or alignment targets, scale mode, reset state, close-up targets, or regional clock selection. The controls become available again when the sequence ends or is exited.

Display, music, and viewing tools

Viewing mode

Press H to hide interface panels for an unobstructed scene. Moving the pointer brings controls back when interaction is available.

Background music

Press M to start or stop ambient music. Track controls appear while music is playing, and your preference is stored in this browser.

Fullscreen and screenshots

Press F for fullscreen. Pause motion, hide the interface, and compose the camera before using your device's screenshot command.

Timer

The timer can play an alarm in the browser. Windows scheduled shutdown is optional and requires the separately verified Solar Time helper.

For steadier performance, Solar Time automatically budgets particles and detail for the device. Closing other graphics-heavy tabs, leaving hardware acceleration enabled, and avoiding extreme browser zoom can also help.

Troubleshooting

The view feels lost or too close

Press 0 to restore the default view. If the lens still feels unusual, right-drag until the magnification indicator returns to a comfortable value.

A texture looks soft

Stay near the selected body for a moment. Detailed textures load progressively and may take longer on a slow connection or a device with a limited graphics budget.

Music or an alarm is silent

Interact with the page once and check the browser tab, operating-system volume, and site autoplay permission. A user gesture is required by many browsers before audio can begin.

A cinematic control is disabled

Finish or exit the current opening, warp, or travel sequence. Controls that could break the camera composition are intentionally restored afterward.

Settings did not persist

Local preferences depend on browser storage. Private browsing, storage cleanup, or restrictive browser settings can reset them. Solar Time does not require an account.
